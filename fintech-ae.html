The Problem

Mortgage delinquency is a core risk metric for financial institutions — but raw loan-level data from Freddie Mac is messy, inconsistently structured, and unusable by business teams without significant transformation work. Most analysts either skip the engineering entirely (building fragile one-off reports) or stop at the raw data layer.

The goal was to simulate how an analytics engineering team would actually solve this: design a layered, tested, documented transformation pipeline that turns raw Freddie Mac acquisition and performance data into reliable mart tables a BI tool can trust — then build the dashboard on top.

Approach
01
Staging Layer — Clean, Predictable Inputs
Built one-to-one staging models for each raw source — renaming columns, explicit type casting, light date parsing. No joins or aggregations at this layer. Staging creates a stable contract between raw sources and downstream models, so any upstream change is caught immediately.
stg_freddie_performancestg_freddie_acquisitionstg_mortgage_ratesType Casting
02
Intermediate Layer — Reusable Business Logic
Centralized two critical pieces of logic: aligning data to the loan-month grain in int_loan_monthly, and defining delinquency flags in int_delinquency_flags_monthly. Keeping logic here prevents duplication across marts and makes the definition of "delinquent" a single source of truth.

03
Mart Layer — BI-Ready Fact Tables
Three mart tables built for distinct analytical needs: monthly delinquency trends, credit band segmentation, and servicer performance. Each mart embeds dimensions directly to simplify BI queries. A reusable macro standardizes credit score banding logic across all three marts.

04
Data Quality Testing & Documentation
24 tests passing across all models — including not_null, unique, referential integrity, and a singular grain test ensuring loan-month uniqueness. All models, columns, and exposures are documented in YAML and rendered in dbt Cloud Catalog with full lineage graphs and test coverage reports.

Design Decisions
Why no incremental models?
Static Freddie Mac dataset — incremental processing adds complexity without benefit on a fixed historical extract.
Why no snapshot models?
No slowly changing dimension need in this dataset — and free-tier BigQuery constraints make snapshot storage wasteful.
Why embed dimensions in marts?
Dimensions (state, channel, credit band) are simple and stable — embedding them keeps BI queries clean without unnecessary joins.
Why a reusable macro for credit banding?
Credit score banding logic is used across multiple marts — a macro ensures one definition, one place to update, zero drift.
Key Findings
Delinquency rates were near zero in January–February 2025, then rose sharply to 0.4–0.5% by May–June — consistent with seasonal mortgage payment stress patterns in the Freddie Mac dataset.
Correspondent channel showed the highest delinquency spike in NY (0.6% in May) compared to Broker and Retail channels — pointing to potential underwriting differences across origination channels worth monitoring.
At the national level, 803K loans were modeled with 2,906 delinquent — a 0.4% overall rate. State-level drill-down reveals NY contributing 29K loans and 89 delinquencies (0.3%), below the national rate.
All 24 data quality tests pass with 64% model coverage — including grain uniqueness tests ensuring the loan-month mart has no duplicates before it reaches the BI layer.
Business Implication

This project demonstrates the core analytics engineering value proposition: raw data that no analyst could safely use becomes a governed, tested, documented mart that any BI tool can trust. The layered architecture means business logic lives in one place — changing the definition of "delinquent" updates every downstream mart automatically.

For a mortgage lender, servicer, or risk team, this platform provides the foundation for ongoing delinquency monitoring, servicer benchmarking, and credit quality segmentation — all built on reproducible dbt models with documented lineage from source to dashboard.
